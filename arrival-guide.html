On the way

Getting there

About an hour and a half from Sydney, and roughly two hours from Canberra. The run down the Hume is straightforward — it's the last twenty minutes, once you turn off toward Penrose, that starts to feel like arriving somewhere.

Access via Kareela Road only

Turn into Victoria Street from Kareela Road — that's the only way in. Some GPS apps try to route you in via Quigg Fire Trail; it crosses a creek and is impassable to cars, so please ignore any suggestion to take it. If your directions app tries to send you that way, stick to Kareela Road instead.

Shop before you turn off

Penrose village has no shop, no fuel and no coffee. The nearest supplies are in Bundanoon, about ten minutes away — The Village Grocer & Store (65 Railway Ave, daily 8.30 am–7.30 pm) and Westside Petroleum (61 Railway Ave, daily 6 am–10 pm). Full supermarkets are 20–25 minutes away in Moss Vale and Mittagong. Our advice: do the big shop on the way in — once you're through the gate you won't want to get back in the car.

Landing properly

Check-in, the gate & your keys

  1. Find the top gate. Coming from Kareela Road, it's directly ahead of you as Victoria Street turns to the left. Please keep this gate shut.
  2. Follow the driveway down. It runs the full length of one side of the property to the homestead at the far end — all-weather and drains well, but take it slowly, it has a few dips.
  3. Park in the shed or on the gravel. There are three bays in the parking shed, or plenty of room on the gravel driveway. Keep to the gravel — the ground right around the house is soft.
  4. Collect the keys from the lock box. It's opposite the front door. Your access code comes through in your Airbnb message before you travel.
  5. Return the keys to the lock box during your stay. That way everyone in your group can come and go on the code, and nobody's left waiting on the verandah.
Arriving after dark

The driveway is dark at night — drive slowly and you'll reach the house. The outdoor lights are on a timer and usually on by nightfall.

Once you're in

Your first half hour

  • In winter, light the wood heater first. It takes a couple of hours to bring the house up to temperature, so start it when you arrive rather than after dinner. Firelighters and kindling are in the bucket beside the fire; the guidebook inside has the full how-to.
  • Put the kettle on. Coffee pods, tea, sugar and basic condiments are in the pantry.
  • Open windows, not doors. Every window has a flyscreen — an open door lets in possums, insects and the occasional more surprising visitor.
  • Relax. Take a drink out to the verandah and enjoy the view — that's rather the point.

Packing list

What we provide · what to bring

We provide

  • All bed linen, beds made up before you arrive, plus bath and hand towels
  • A fully equipped kitchen for nine, and a picnic set
  • Coffee pods, tea, sugar, salt, pepper, oil and basic condiments
  • Firewood and kindling for the wood heater and fire pits
  • A cot and a high chair, whenever you need them
  • Games, books, puzzles and outdoor games
  • Wi-Fi, smart TV and a Bluetooth soundbar
  • Washing machine, dryer and clothesline

Please bring

  • Your own towels for the dam and outdoors — house towels stay indoors
  • Groceries — there's no shop in Penrose
  • Closed walking shoes for the hundred acres of paddock and bush
  • Warm layers, even in summer — we're at altitude and evenings cool sharply
  • Swimmers, if it's warm — the dam is a proper swimming hole
  • Sun protection, and repellent for dusk on the verandah
  • Your own coffee if you're particular — the machine takes Aldi pods only

The short version

House rules

Treat it like a friend's place. The full version is in the guidebook inside — here's what matters most before you arrive.

  • Nine guests maximum, and nobody extra staying overnight
  • No parties or events. It's a quiet valley with neighbours who value it
  • No pets. The wildlife is the whole point of the place
  • No smoking indoors
  • Only drive on the driveway — the tracks are for walking
  • Leave gates as you found them
  • Supervise children at the dam, every time

Living rural

A few things worth knowing

A rural property runs a little differently to a house in town. Nothing here is difficult — just worth knowing before you arrive.

The water is rainwater

Every drop comes off the roof into the tanks. It's filtered and drinkable — use it as you would at home, with half an eye on the fact that it isn't limitless.

The toilets are septic

Only pee, poo and toilet paper. No wipes, ever — even ones labelled "flushable".

Driving at dusk

Kangaroos and wombats move at dawn and dusk, often without warning. Between about 4 pm and 8 pm, slow down on the local roads — if you see one, expect more behind it.

Before lighting any outdoor fire

Check for a Total Fire Ban — the Fires Near Me NSW app, or rfs.nsw.gov.au. Our district is the Southern Highlands. Check on the day, not the night before.

Phones and internet

Coverage is generally good here and across most of the Southern Highlands, though the last stretch of the drive in can be patchy — worth downloading directions or this page before you lose signal.
